## 1. Exact count certificate: chronology is connectivity in reverse ancestry

For oriented edges let m_uv be nonnegative integers. Write out_v=sum_w m_vw, in_v=sum_u m_uv, and A(m)_v=2 out_v-in_v. Let W consist of q, every initially occupied vertex, and every endpoint of a positive-count arc. Call m **root-connected** if each v in W has a directed path to q along positive-count arcs (the zero-length path at q is allowed).

**Theorem44 (exact connected balance).** For every c in N^V, q in V, integer k>=1, and prescribed finite move multiset m, the following are equivalent:

1. There is a legal sequence using exactly m_uv copies of every u->v and ending at exactly k e_q.
2. c=k e_q+A(m), and m is root-connected.

Consequently c clears at q iff these conditions hold for some m with k=1; c stacks at q iff they hold for some m and some k>=1. The root existential comes OUTSIDE the entire certificate. Every witness consumes every input; no leftover inequality appears. Summing gives |c|=k+sum m, so each witness count is bounded by input mass, although the theorem has no fixed mass bound.

Proof of necessity. Track all labelled ancestry trees of the k final tokens. Each original leaf and each internal move lies on a path to a root labelled q. Parent labels are move destinations and child labels are sources, so every label used in a tree has a positive-arc directed path to q, apart from a singleton root. Counting moves gives the exact balance. This applies to every forest component, not only a chosen marked tree.

For sufficiency use the following established commutative-grammar ordering theorem, with its proof actually inspected: a finite multiset of productions, each consuming ONE nonterminal and producing finitely many nonterminals plus terminal labels, can be ordered from a pending vector s to a pending vector t iff its counts balance and every used nonterminal is reachable from supp(s) in the production dependency graph. See SRC-0036, Kopczynski, Theorem2.7 and Theorem2.9/Corollary2.10, PDF pp8–10 (arXiv1501.04245v3). This is a known theorem, not a new pebbling scheduling principle or the invalid forward multi-input balance relaxation.

Take one nonterminal X_v per vertex. Use m_uv copies of X_v -> X_u X_u and c(v) copies of X_v -> terminal_v. Start with k copies of X_q and finish with no nonterminal. The number of uses of X_v is in_v+c(v), the number newly generated is 2 out_v, and the starting count is k delta_vq. Thus the exact grammar balance is precisely condition2. Its dependency arcs are v->u whenever m_uv>0: reachability from q is precisely root-connectedness. The ordering theorem therefore builds a complete derivation forest from k root copies with terminal vector c. Give each terminal occurrence a distinct original input label. Reverse each ancestry tree by postorder: both child outputs are at u and their parent is at adjacent v, so every production is a legal pebbling move. Disjoint leaf sets let all k trees be evaluated without disturbing one another. Their counts are exactly m and their final pile exactly k e_q. QED.

The cited ordering proof handles multiple pending roots and terminal productions; no new proof obligation is hidden in the change from a tree to a forest. Its one-nonterminal hypothesis holds in REVERSE ancestry, not in the original move system. Deleting cycles from forward counts is neither used nor legitimate. Cyclic count supports remain permitted, including pure6 clearing on K3. A rank certificate 0<=rank(v)<=|W|-1, rank(q)=0, and for every other used v a positive arc v->w with smaller rank encodes root-connectedness by finitely many alternatives.

**Explicit refutation of weaker connectivity.** On K3 with vertices0,1,2, take c=(0,1,3), q=2, k=1, and m_20=m_01=m_10=1, all other counts zero. Exact balance holds and the underlying undirected count support is connected. The only prescribed first move2->0 produces (1,1,1), which is terminal. Independently, every unrestricted first move is2->0 or2->1. The former loses; the latter gives (0,2,1), then either1->0 gives (1,0,1) terminal, or1->2 gives2e2, whose last move ends at0 or1. Thus c CANNOT clear at2, though it clears elsewhere. It can also stack at2 in two pebbles. This refutes only weakly connected rooted-clear balance, not ASH*, not unrooted clearing, and not Theorem44. The old disconnected K3 all-ones balance obstruction is preserved, not rediscovered as a universal disproof.

    f_v in {0,1}, sum_v f_v=1, f_v=0 unless av is an edge;

Proof. A fragment's every proper boundary occurrence is a leaf hole; paired child labels therefore force holes to arise only from a->v productions and in pairs, giving h=2 sum z. ## 3. Graph-constrained cones: Route A gets an exact dual problem

For W containing q and a directed in-arborescence T of G[W] pointing towards q, give every T arc initial count1, every other arc in G[W] initial count0, and add arbitrary nonnegative integer counts x on ALL arcs in G[W]. Put

    c=k e_q + A(1_T+x), c>=0, c outside W=0.

**Corollary46 (exact finite union of connected integer cones).** For fixed G,q,k this union over ALL W,T is exactly the set of configurations that can reach k e_q. For clearing fix k=1; for stacking allow k=1,2,... . W={q}, T empty includes the initially terminal pile. No empty forest is admitted.

Proof. Every displayed count vector contains an in-arborescence, hence every used vertex reaches q, and44 realizes the exact input. Conversely a44 witness's positive support has paths to q; selecting one decreasing-distance outgoing arc at each nonroot vertex gives an in-arborescence T. Every T arc has count>=1, so subtracting1_T leaves nonnegative x. The initially occupied coordinates outside the positive arc support can only be q by balance, so W is valid. QED.

This is a finite arithmetic union for each graph, with integer generators allowed to have negative coordinates and then an exact nonnegative-input intersection. It is not an abstract upward monomial ideal: the graph edges, fixed root/pile, a required arborescence, and exact equality are retained. The input support may be smaller than W because internal transit vertices can start empty. All roots are finally unioned, never minimized separately inside different components.

Thus Route A's universal remaining obligation is concrete: prove that the maximum mass outside the union of these connected integer cones, over ALL eligible graphs and all final roots/piles, has an AS representative. Equivalently prove the original |b|+min_q A_goal(s,b,q,r)-3<=L_AS(G) for every positive last-losing arbitrary buffer ray. All support/residue classes, zero bits, exact consumption and carries survive; no upper domination follows just from the finite representation.

An ordinary real-cone dual is inadequate. Each edge gives 3e_v=A(e_uv+2e_vu); hence a positive coordinate axis lies in the rational move cone even where its small integer pile cannot clear. The signed3 lattice obstruction persists on bipartite used subgraphs, while a non-bipartite connected support has trivial move-invariant quotient (S00416). Neither observation controls integer holes plus root connectivity. The finite rank disjunction is the additional graph-realizability constraint that arbitrary-ideal arguments lacked; proving that it forces AS extremality is still a missing theorem.

## 4. Complete forest reconstruction and critical certificate restrictions

**Corollary47 (root-padding cancellation).** Suppose c+p e_q legally reaches k e_q using counts m, with integers p>=0 and k>p. Then c legally reaches (k-p)e_q using the SAME move counts, in a possibly different order. This holds for every finite G and nonnegative c.

Proof. Subtract p e_q from the exact44 balance, obtaining c=(k-p)e_q+A(m). Every used vertex apart from q is unchanged; q remains the prescribed nonempty root. Root-connectedness is therefore unchanged, so44 reconstructs a forest on all original inputs of c. This is exact cancellation of root INPUT and root OUTPUT, not arbitrary input removal from a given tree. QED.

For a loser c, any winning c+e_s certificate rooted at s must therefore have FINAL PILE1; if it has a different root the conclusion does not apply. Every winning c+3e_s certificate rooted at s must finish with at most3. This constrains simultaneous conductor-tightness witnesses and distinguishes trees from forests. A same-site winning forest cannot simply be assumed from an existential-root win. CAM-S010-02 challenges whether criticality supplies that missing alignment. The marked walk and complete unmarked forest of39 remain indispensable.

**Proposition48 (exact reversal/excision test and critical barriers).** Fix a44 certificate of c at q,k. Along a simple path v0,...,vd, suppose each arc vi->v(i+1) has positive count. Subtract one copy of each such arc and add one of each reverse arc, giving m'. Then

    k e_q+A(m') = c-3e_v0+3e_vd.

If this input is nonnegative and m' remains root-connected, it legally reaches the same q,k. In particular counts at least2 on all removed arcs guarantee connectivity survives: every old positive arc remains, and the new arcs introduce no new vertices. The single-edge version requires no tree/component/frontier truncation.

Proof. For an edge uv, A(e_vu-e_uv)=3(e_v-e_u). Sum along the path; intermediate coordinates cancel. Root-connectedness and44 then give the complete reconstruction of ALL final components. With counts>=2 the positive support contains every original arc, so all old root paths survive. QED.

Now let c be a global largest loser maximizing Q=sum c(v)^2. If s,t are adjacent, c(s)>=3, c(t)>=c(s), set d=c-3e_s+3e_t. The mass is unchanged and

    Q(d)-Q(c)=6(c(t)-c(s))+18>0.

Thus d MUST win, otherwise c was not a Q maximizer. For EVERY winning44 certificate of d, the reverse arc t->s has count0, OR it has count1 and is essential for connection to that certificate's root. Count>=2, or count1 with its deletion still root-connected, would reconstruct c by the previous test and contradict its loss. If the count1 edge is essential, after deleting it there is a nonempty sink strongly connected component S not containing q, containing t, with all its original exits to the remaining graph consisting solely of that one t->s copy. Adding s->t enters S and cannot restore an exit. This is an exact root-separating cut obstruction, not a bound on damaged forest count. For nonadjacent s,t use a path and retain the full resulting connectivity test; at least one required count or surviving root path fails. Heavy donor count2 lies outside this3-transfer argument and remains an explicit obligation.

The sink-cut assertion follows by considering vertices that lose all paths to q when the arc is deleted; they include t and form a closed region after deletion. Following its condensation graph to a sink gives the asserted S; any original outgoing arc except t->s would persist and contradict being a sink. A lost component without t would have lost no exiting edge, impossible since the original support was root-connected.

There is a stronger root-aligned case. If q=v0, reversing any available simple path v0->...->vd always preserves root connection: an old root path broken at a deleted vi->v(i+1) can instead take the new reverse path vi->...->v0. Every old used vertex therefore still reaches q. Thus in a winning certificate for d=c-3e_s+3e_t rooted at t, NO positive-count directed path t->s can exist, or reversing it would reconstruct c. The donor must lie outside the root's strongly connected component in every such certificate. This is a proved no-return restriction on exact consuming certificates, not an imported target no-merging theorem with unused tokens.

An occupied-source tightness certificate for c+3e_s obeys a related anti-excision rule. For each neighbor u, removing two s->u and one u->s counts would subtract3e_s from the exact input and preserve q,k. If those counts are available and the remaining support is root-connected, c would win. Hence EVERY such extension certificate fails availability or connectivity. In particular it cannot have both m_su>=3 and m_us>=2, because removing2/1 would leave all old arcs positive. This condition is necessary for ANY loser, and therefore for critical tightness; it does not imply criticality or ASH*.

These statements replace incomplete surgery by an exact reconstruction predicate. A removed marked leaf can still damage three trees and expose arbitrarily many outputs;44 reconstructs from the full actual frontier if and only if its new counts and root connection exist. It does not promise those counts exist. The new positive result is the reconstruction/cancellation criterion; the remaining selection step is to prove that all the simultaneous thin-cut/no-count obstructions cannot coexist at a non-AS global Q maximum, or to realize them in a genuine ASH* counterexample.

## 5. Minimal counterexamples: only justified reductions

For each goal separately, if counterexamples exist choose G by the well-founded lexicographic order (number of vertices, number of edges), with finite isomorphism classes at each pair. Threshold finiteness is credited to the source and independently established by S00627; terminal all-ones gives g>n. A smaller eligible graph satisfies that goal's ASH* by minimality, not by an unaudited computation.

**Proposition49 (threshold-saturated quotient restriction).** Let f:G->H be a surjective simple-graph homomorphism between eligible graphs for the SAME goal. Then g(G)>=g(H). If g(G)=g(H) and H has a critical AS loser, G has one as well. Therefore a vertex-minimal universal counterexample satisfies g(G)>g(H) for EVERY eligible proper vertex quotient that is a true simple-graph homomorphism.

Proof. Lift any H configuration by choosing one preimage for each vertex and placing its whole pile there. Legal moves push along f to legal moves: an edge cannot collapse to a loop under a simple-graph homomorphism. Stacked/cleared goals push to the same goal, with the same positive final pile. If every mass-t G input wins, every H input wins, giving the correct threshold direction; source April7.1(2)'s printed opposite direction was already repaired in S009. Now lift a losing AS configuration d on H. A winning sequence upstairs would push to a win for d, impossible. The chosen-preimage lift is AS and has exactly the same mass. Equality of thresholds makes that mass g(G)-1. The minimal-counterexample conclusion follows because H has fewer vertices. QED.

Identifying ANY two nonadjacent vertices and keeping the union of their neighbors gives a valid simple homomorphic quotient, preserves connectivity, and cannot make a non-bipartite G bipartite (a homomorphism into a bipartite graph would give a bipartition upstairs). Its order remains>=2 for an eligible noncomplete G. Thus a minimal counterexample must lose threshold strictly under each such identification. This is a proved structural restriction, not a claim that these quotients always preserve a threshold. Complete graphs and previously audited complete multipartite graphs already satisfy ASH* with credited thresholds.

Adjacent contraction is NOT this reduction: its edge would become a loop and a move would become a one-token deletion, absent from the legal model. Edge deletion gives the opposite threshold monotonicity and may introduce a new AS loser destroyed by restoring the edge. If an eligible edge-deleted H has the same threshold as a counterexample G, every critical AS loser on H must become winning on G, and EVERY G witness for it must use the restored edge. That necessary fact does not rule out G. Attachment raises thresholds and unrooted block minima cannot combine roots;45 gives exact messages but no critical-AS preservation theorem for attachments/cactus joins. No unproved articulation/bridge exclusion is claimed.

**Theorem50 (three-pile deletion reflection).** On ANY finite undirected simple graph, if d legally reaches exactly3e_r, then for EVERY initially occupied w, d-e_w legally reaches exactly2e_w. Every remaining input is consumed. Thus every one-pebble deletion is stackable, regardless of the chosen original final root r.

First proof, complete labelled reflection. Choose any such legal forest and mark one original input at w. The three final roots partition ALL original labels. Two final trees are unmarked; the marked tree's unique leaf-to-root path has labels w=v0,...,vm=r. At each path merge its sibling is an unmarked tree ending at vi. These siblings, together with the two other final trees, partition exactly d-e_w. Replay ALL these disjoint trees: there are two designated tokens at r and one at each vi for i=0,...,m-1. Starting with the two at r, move along the REVERSE walk r->v(m-1)->...->v0. At each intermediate site the arriving token pairs with that site's designated sibling token; the final arrival at w pairs with the final sibling but is NOT moved again. Exactly two remain at w. Repeated graph vertices cause no problem: token designations distinguish sibling outputs. If m=0 the marked tree is a singleton at w=r and replaying the two unmarked trees already gives2e_w. Nothing else survives. QED.

Second derivation/adversarial check using44. Let m be the original3-pile counts and P the full marked forward WALK count vector, with multiplicities. Define m'=m-P+reverse(P), nonnegative because every marked production was a distinct original occurrence. For each arc, A(reverse-P)=3(e_r-e_w), telescoping along the walk, so

    d-e_w = 2e_w + A(m').

Every old used vertex had a positive path to r. If that path loses an arc vi->v(i+1), stop at vi and take the reversed marked prefix to w; otherwise reach r and take the entire reversed marked walk. Hence every used vertex still reaches w. Removing the marked input introduces no other new input label. By44 the same transformed count multiset consumes d-e_w into2e_w. This proof retains whole forests and arbitrary repeated spines; it supplies a distinct exact reconstruction check on the first derivation, not an independent human/kernel review.
