## 3. Neighborhood concentration: an actual global comparison step

Write A(m)=2out-in, c=f+A(m). The audited exact count theorem reconstructs ALL input into the nonempty terminal f precisely when every used vertex reaches supp(f). Its generic scheduling mechanism is credited to labelled ancestry and SRC-0036, not claimed as new.

**Theorem63 (open-neighborhood even concentration preserves loss).** Let s!=t in finite connected simple G, N(t) subseteq N(s). Let c>=0, c(t)>=2, integer a>=1 with c(s)>=2a, and put d=c-2a e_s+2a e_t. For EITHER goal, if d wins then c wins. Consequently a losing c remains losing after this concentration. CLEAR here concerns individual exact singleton reachability even on a bipartite graph; a universal CLEAR threshold is used only on eligible graphs. The final root is unrestricted, including s,t and all other sites.

Proof. Normalize a winning STACK terminal pile to k in{1,2,3} at its root q by the credited positive-base +3 operation; for CLEAR take k=1. Let m be its complete count certificate, so d=k e_q+A(m), with every used label reaching q.

If q!=t, its t balance is 2out_t-in_t=d(t)=c(t)+2a>=2a+2, hence out_t>=a+1. Because t is occupied in d it has a simple positive directed path P to q. Protect ONE copy of the first t arc of P. Choose any a other outgoing copies at t. If q=t, the balance gives 2out_t=d(t)-k+in_t>=2a-1, hence integral out_t>=a; protect no arc and choose any a outgoing copies.

For each selected t->v copy substitute s->v. The neighborhood inclusion guarantees a true edge and excludes s~t (otherwise s would belong to N(s)). The resulting counts m' are nonnegative and satisfy A(m')=A(m)+2a(e_s-e_t). Thus c=k e_q+A(m'). Every old root path avoids outgoing t when q=t. When q!=t, every old root path either survives or first reaches t at a removed t arc, and can then follow the protected simple P; only its first arc leaves t, so its entire P survives. Thus every old used vertex still reaches q. Any newly used s has a selected new outgoing arc to an old used head v, whose path to q was just retained. No other new label appears. All original c inputs and every count are therefore root-connected. Exact reconstruction gives c->kq, proving its goal. QED.

Boundary: no transfer of a single odd token, no zero/heavy receiver1, no adjacent closed-neighborhood substitution or fixed-root conductor equality is asserted. Connectivity and the protected copy, rather than balance alone, are essential. Normalized k<=3 is what supplies enough outgoing copies even when q=t and c(t)=2. This is an unrooted winner implication with a reconstructed actual certificate; it resolves the donor-root alignment problem at the stated neighborhood-inclusion scope.
