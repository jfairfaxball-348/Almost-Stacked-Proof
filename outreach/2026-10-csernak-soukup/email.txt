Subject: The printed Almost Stacked Hypothesis fails on P4: a small example and a suggested reformulation

Dear Tamás Csernák and Lajos Soukup,

I have been reading your papers "Stacking and clearing in graph pebbling" (arXiv:2604.22341, cited below as [A]) and "The Almost Stacked Hypothesis" (arXiv:2610.09650v1, cited below as [O]) with great interest. I believe there is a small gap between the Almost Stacked Hypothesis as printed ([O, Hypothesis 3.2] = [A, Hypothesis 9.1]) and the statement your atlas code checks. In case it is useful before a revision, here are the details.

1. The printed statement fails on P4.

[O, Hypothesis 3.2(1)] says that stack(G) is the least integer t ≥ 2 such that every almost stacked configuration in Conf_t(G) is stackable. Let P4 = v1–v2–v3–v4 and t = 12.
- All 32 almost stacked configurations of size 12 are stackable.
- (v1, v2, v3, v4) = (1, 0, 2, 9) is not stackable. Its reachable set has 50 configurations, is closed under moves, and contains no stacked configuration.
- For every t with 2 ≤ t ≤ 11 some almost stacked configuration of size t is not stackable.

So the least t in Hypothesis 3.2(1) is 12, while stack(P4) = 15 by [O, Theorem 4.4] (= [A, Theorem 7.4]). In fact, since 12 > |V(P4)|, the size-12 example together with [O, Lemma 4.7] and (6.1) already gives stack(P4) > 12.

The same happens for clearing, [O, Hypothesis 3.2(2)], on the bull graph, which is non-bipartite: a triangle x, y, z with a pendant vertex p on y and a pendant vertex q on z.
- All 80 almost stacked configurations of size 12 are clearable.
- (p, q, x, y, z) = (9, 1, 0, 2, 0) is not clearable. Its reachable set has 96 configurations, is closed, and contains no cleared configuration.
- The least t in Hypothesis 3.2(2) is again 12, while clear = 15 by exhaustive computation.

2. Why the atlas did not catch it.

[O, Lemma 4.7] makes "every configuration of size t is stackable" monotone in t once t > |V|. Nothing similar holds for almost stacked configurations alone: a move from an almost stacked configuration need not produce an almost stacked one. So stackability of every almost stacked configuration of size t does not carry over to size t + 1.

Your functions Pebbling.is_critical_stacked_almost_stacked and Pebbling.is_critical_clear_almost_stacked (github.com/lajossoukup/pebbling) check whether the witness set returned by stacking_number() (resp. clearing_number()) contains an almost stacked configuration. That witness set is the set of non-stackable (non-clearable) configurations of size stack(G) − 1 (clear(G) − 1). This is a different statement, and I suspect it is the intended one.

3. A suggested reformulation (ASH*).

stack(G) is the least t₀ ≥ 2 such that every almost stacked configuration of every size t ≥ t₀ is stackable. Likewise for clear(G) when G is non-bipartite.

For |V(G)| ≥ 2 this is equivalent to: some almost stacked configuration of size stack(G) − 1 is not stackable (resp. of size clear(G) − 1, not clearable). In other words, the largest size of a non-stackable configuration is attained by an almost stacked one. This seems to me to match the intuition of [O, §3.1], and it is exactly what the atlas code tests.

The applications in [O, §4–5] go through unchanged under ASH*:
- Theorems 4.10 and 4.11 apply to almost stacked configurations of every size at or above the bound.
- For trees, Theorem 5.3 together with inequality (5.9) does the same for every size ≥ estim(T).

The §6 results (Corollary 6.6 and Theorems 6.9, 6.11, 6.13) use almost stacked stackability only at size |V| + 1. As far as I can see, under ASH* they would need an additional argument covering larger sizes.

4. Evidence for ASH*.

I have no counterexample to ASH*. I checked stacking and clearing separately, with code independent of yours. ASH* holds on:
- every connected graph with at most 6 vertices;
- every connected 7-vertex graph whose threshold is at most 55. This is all but 8 of the 853 graphs for stacking, and all but 3 of the 809 non-bipartite graphs for clearing; the remaining graphs were not decided. This is consistent with your atlas report.
- every connected 8-vertex graph whose threshold is at most 26 (10,225 graphs for stacking, 10,052 non-bipartite graphs for clearing). Graphs with larger thresholds were not decided.

It also holds for every finite tree T with at least two vertices:
- The witness in [O, Theorem 5.2] is almost stacked, of size estim(T) − 1.
- stack(T) = estim(T) for |V(T)| ≥ 2 is proved in my paper "The stacking number of a tree" (arXiv:2609.31811, September 2026; v2 cites [O]), with a Lean formalisation. This is your tree conjecture [A, Conjecture 10.3] for trees with at least two vertices. With the convention estim = 2 for one vertex in [O, Definition 5.1], it gives [O, Conjecture 5.6] in full.

It also holds for complete bipartite graphs K_{a,b} with a + b ≥ 3. Let α = max(a, b). Put 2α + 1 pebbles on one vertex w of the larger side and one pebble on each other vertex of that side. This gives an almost stacked configuration of size 3α, which is not stackable by a mod-3 weight argument with a short counting step. Together with [A, Theorem 8.2], this gives ASH*.
